Financial Empowerment Initiative

Understand your credit. Correct the record. Build stability.

Sapientia Foundation teaches people how credit and debt actually work, helps them find and fix the errors on their own credit records, and gives them free tools to act on their rights — in the communities the system overlooks.

Our Mission

Free, non-commercial financial education — and the tools to use it.

The Fair Credit & Debt Program is purely educational and self-directed. The Foundation charges consumers nothing, makes no loans, does not negotiate or settle debts on anyone's behalf, and neither pays nor receives commissions or referral fees. Our only interest is an informed, financially stable community.

Sapientia Foundation is an irrevocable non-profit charitable trust, organized under the laws of Delaware and recognized as a 501(c)(3) public charity. EIN 39-4961628.

Why This Matters

A damaged credit record is a quiet tax on the households least able to afford it.

A credit record follows a person into every major financial decision — renting a home, buying a car, getting a loan, setting an insurance premium, sometimes getting a job. Most people do not know their rights under the Fair Credit Reporting Act, and commercial "credit repair" charges high fees for work a person can do themselves once someone shows them how.

The Program

Four ways we help people act on their own rights.

Community workshops

A short series covering how to read a credit report and score; the Fair Credit Reporting Act and the right to dispute; budgeting and debt payoff; medical and student debt; collections and time limits; spotting predatory loans; and building credit safely from a thin file.

Free guided self-help tool

An online, plain-language application that helps a person read their own credit reports, flag likely errors, and prepare their own dispute, validation, and goodwill letters. The consumer remains the author and sender of every letter and keeps every record.

Document-prep clinics

Drop-in sessions where trained educators help participants pull their reports, use the tool, understand the results, and organize their own follow-up. Free and offered in multiple languages.

Digital library & partner training

Curriculum modules and short videos in several languages, available to anyone, plus train-the-trainer sessions so reentry programs, housing counselors, workforce boards, libraries, and legal-aid offices can deliver the program themselves.

Who We Serve

People with a thin or damaged credit file — and the least room to absorb the cost.

Priority geography is Northern Virginia and the surrounding region. The self-help tool and digital curriculum are available nationally, online, at no charge.

  • Low- and moderate-income adults
  • Returning citizens rebuilding after incarceration
  • Immigrants and refugees establishing credit history
  • Young adults and students
  • People carrying medical or collections debt
  • Survivors of identity theft and financial abuse
  • Anyone with a thin or damaged credit file

Safeguards

This is not commercial credit repair.

No fees, no loans, no debt negotiation

No consumer fees, no loans, no settling debts on anyone's behalf, no commissions or referral fees. 501(c)(3) organizations are excluded from the federal Credit Repair Organizations Act and from most state credit-services-organization statutes; counsel confirms each state's treatment before the program operates there.

Your data stays yours

No product sales, no lender or carrier sponsorship, no lead generation. Personal data is minimized, stored securely, and never shared with lenders, insurers, credit bureaus, or third parties.

Foundation-owned tools

The self-help tool, curriculum, and letter library are developed and owned by the Foundation. All published materials are reviewed by counsel before use.

Board independence

A majority of voting trustees will be independent. Conflict-of-interest and disclosure policies govern all related-party matters. Participants who need licensed advice are referred to multiple independent professionals or to legal aid.

Partners

You bring the space and the trust. We bring the curriculum and the tools.

The Foundation is building agreements with public libraries, faith congregations, reentry and workforce programs, housing counseling agencies, and legal-aid organizations. Partners provide space, referrals, and subject-matter review; Sapientia provides curriculum, trained educators, and the free tools — plus train-the-trainer sessions so your staff can deliver the program themselves.

About the Foundation

A charitable trust for education and economic opportunity.

Sapientia Foundation is a charitable trust established to advance education and economic opportunity. Its Declaration of Trust expressly authorizes programs "for education or betterment" and "for economic relief or hardship." The Foundation delivers direct community education rather than grantmaking, is governed by a Board of Trustees that meets at least quarterly, and requires unanimous trustee consent for financial commitments.
