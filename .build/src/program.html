The Fair Credit & Debt Program

Free, non-commercial financial education and self-help tools. Every service is educational and self-directed — no consumer fees, no loans, no debt negotiation, no commissions or referral fees.

Purpose

Teach how credit and debt work, then hand people the tools to act.

The commercial "credit repair" industry charges high fees for work a person can do themselves once someone shows them how. This program shows them how — and gives them the letter templates, checklists, and guided tool to do it, keeping every record in their own hands.

Community workshops

A short series covering how to read a credit report and score; the Fair Credit Reporting Act and the right to dispute; budgeting and debt payoff; medical and student debt; collections and time limits; spotting predatory loans and fees; and building credit safely from a thin file.

Free guided self-help tool

An online, plain-language application that helps a person read their own credit reports, flag likely errors, and prepare their own dispute, validation, and goodwill letters. The consumer remains the author and sender of every letter and keeps every record. Open the tool →

Document-prep clinics

Drop-in sessions where trained educators help participants pull their reports, use the tool, understand the results, and organize their own follow-up. Free and offered in multiple languages. Find a clinic →

Digital library & partner training

Curriculum modules and short videos in several languages, available to anyone, plus train-the-trainer sessions so reentry programs, housing counselors, workforce boards, libraries, and legal-aid offices can deliver the program themselves. Partner with us →

How a participant moves through it

Learn, review, act, follow up.

  1. Learn the basics in a workshop

    Understand what a credit report is, what actually affects a score, and what the Fair Credit Reporting Act entitles you to do.

  2. Pull and review your own reports

    Use the free tool or a document-prep clinic to read your reports and flag entries that look inaccurate, unverifiable, or out of date.

  3. Prepare your own letters

    The tool drafts dispute, validation, and goodwill letters from your notes. You review, edit, and send them yourself.

  4. Track responses and escalate

    Log what each bureau or creditor says back, and prepare the appropriate follow-up letter if an item is verified without support or left unchanged.

This is not commercial credit repair. The Foundation provides education and self-directed tools only. 501(c)(3) organizations are excluded from the federal Credit Repair Organizations Act and from most state credit-services-organization statutes; counsel confirms each state's treatment before the program operates there.
Expected impact

Knowledge that compounds, records that get corrected.

Participants show measurable gains in credit and debt knowledge, dispute and remove inaccurate items from their own records, see improved credit standing over time, and avoid the fees of commercial credit repair. A permanent bank of free, plain-language curriculum, videos, and tools stays available to the community and to partner organizations.
